4.5 Sharing and Copying AMIs

AMIs are regional resources; they exist and can only be used within the AWS Region where they were created. Sharing and copying are the two primary mechanisms to make your custom AMIs accessible across multiple accounts and geographic regions.
🔄 sharing 📋 copying 🌍 cross-region
📖

Sharing vs. Copying

The decision between sharing and copying is a fundamental architectural choice that balances cost, management overhead, and security.

🔑 Sharing
Granting launch permissions to other AWS accounts. The AMI remains in your account, and you retain full ownership and control.
✅ No additional storage cost
✅ Centralized management
✅ Deregistering revokes access
📋 Copying
Creates a new, independent AMI in a different Region or account. A complete replica with its own lifecycle.
💰 Additional storage cost
🔄 Independent lifecycle
📊 Decentralized management

Sharing an AMI

Sharing an AMI grants another AWS account permission to launch instances from your AMI. This is the preferred pattern for most cross-account use cases because it is cost-effective and centralizes management.

Key Characteristics:
  • 🏷️ Ownership: The source account retains ownership.
  • 💰 Cost: No additional storage costs.
  • 📊 Management: Centrally manage permissions; retire AMIs by deregistering them from the source account.
  • 📋 Permissions: Launch permissions are not automatically copied when the AMI is later copied.

Making an AMI Public:
  • Available in Community AMIs
  • ⚠️ Encrypted Volumes: AMIs with encrypted volumes cannot be made public
  • 🔒 Sensitive Data: Be careful not to expose sensitive data
  • 💰 Billing: You are not billed when others launch instances from your public AMI
🔑 sharing 🌍 public 💰 free
📋

Copying an AMI

Copying an AMI creates a new, independent AMI. You must copy an AMI to a new Region before you can use it there because AMIs are region-specific.

Cross-Region and Cross-Account Copy Strategy:
  1. Source Account: Share the AMI with the target account
  2. Target Account: Copy the shared AMI from source Region to target Region
  3. Post-Copy: Review and update region-specific configurations

Considerations and Best Practices

Permissions:
  • IAM Policies: Resource-level permissions for CopyImage (since October 2024)
  • Shared AMIs: Destination account must have "Create volume" permission

🔐 Encrypted AMIs:
  • KMS Key Permissions: Destination account must be granted access to the source KMS key
  • Update the KMS key policy to allow the destination account to use the key

🏷️ Watermarks:
  • Up to 5 structured identifiers can be attached to private AMIs
  • Persistent across copies and sharing
  • Helps in tracking provenance and enforcing governance

💰 Cost:
  • Sharing: No additional cost
  • Copying: Standard storage and data transfer fees apply
  • Time-based AMI copy operations incur additional charges

Key Takeaways

  • AMIs are regional resources. Use copying to make an AMI available in another Region or to give another account ownership.

  • Use sharing for cost-effective, centralized access across accounts. Sharing is free and allows the source account to maintain control.

  • Copying an AMI creates an independent resource in the destination account. You will incur storage costs for the copied AMI's snapshots.

  • Copying a shared AMI is the standard pattern for account-to-account migration across Regions.

  • For encrypted AMIs, you must configure KMS key policies to grant cross-account access before the copy will succeed.

  • AMI watermarks persist across copy and sharing operations, providing a way to track provenance.

  • Public AMIs can be shared with all AWS accounts, but they cannot contain encrypted volumes or product codes.

Review Questions

Q1: What is the difference between sharing an AMI and copying an AMI?
Answer: Sharing grants launch permissions to another account without creating a new copy; the source account retains ownership and incurs no additional costs. Copying creates an entirely new AMI in the destination Region or account, which then owns the copy, but you pay for the copy's storage. Sharing is preferred for centralized management, while copying is necessary for global deployment.
Q2: Can you copy an AMI with encrypted snapshots to another account?
Answer: Yes, but you must first configure the KMS key policy in the source account to allow the destination account access to use the key for decryption and re-encryption. Additionally, the IAM user performing the copy needs permissions on both the KMS key and the AMI copy action.
Q3: How can you transfer an AMI from an account in the Mumbai region to an account in the London region?
Answer: You cannot transfer an AMI directly. The process requires two steps: First, in the source account (Mumbai), share the AMI with the target account. Second, in the target account (London), copy the shared AMI from the Mumbai region to the London region. Review region-specific dependencies after the copy.
Q4: What happens to launch permissions when you copy an AMI?
Answer: Launch permissions are not copied automatically to the new AMI. After the copy completes, you must explicitly set launch permissions (e.g., making it private, sharing it, or making it public) for the newly created AMI in the destination Region.
Q5: What are the cost implications of sharing and copying AMIs?
Answer: Sharing an AMI does not incur additional cost. Copying an AMI does incur costs: you pay standard EBS snapshot storage fees for the new AMI's backing snapshots in the destination Region, and data transfer fees apply for cross-Region copies. Time-based AMI copies incur additional charges beyond storage and transfer.
