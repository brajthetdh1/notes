7.2 Reserved Instances (RIs)

📖 Complete Theory

Reserved Instances (RIs) are a pricing model that provides significant discounts — up to 75% compared to On-Demand pricing — in exchange for a commitment to a specific instance configuration for a 1-year or 3-year term.

An RI is not a physical instance; rather, it is a billing discount applied to On-Demand Instances that match certain attributes such as instance type, Region, and operating system.
📊 RIs 💰 up to 75% 📋 commitment

7.2.1 Standard RIs

📖 Complete Theory: The Deep Discount, Rigid Commitment

Standard Reserved Instances are the most cost-effective way to commit to AWS compute resources. They offer the highest possible discount compared to On-Demand pricing — up to 75% — by requiring you to lock in the specific attributes of your EC2 usage for a 1-year or 3-year term.

The Core Trade-off: The significant discount comes at the cost of rigidity. A Standard RI is a billing discount applied to On-Demand instances that match a very specific configuration.

⚠️ Real-World Impact:
  • ✅ Good: Run m5.large in us-east-1 for 12 months → Full discount
  • ❌ Bad: Switch to m5.2xlarge → Only the m5.large portion gets the discount
  • ❌ Worse: Migrate to eu-west-1 → RI cannot be used; pay On-Demand + unused RI
  • ❌ Worst: Change to c5.large → Discount completely lost
📊 Standard RI 💰 up to 75% 🔒 rigid
🔄

7.2.2 Convertible RIs

📖 Complete Theory: The Flexible Commitment

Convertible Reserved Instances (CRIs) offer a middle ground between the deep discounts of Standard RIs and the flexibility of Savings Plans. They provide a significant discount — up to 66% off On-Demand pricing — in exchange for a 1-year or 3-year commitment, while giving you the ability to change instance attributes as your needs evolve.

🔄 The Exchange Mechanism:
  • Initiate an exchange request via Console or API
  • AWS calculates the number of new RIs based on list values
  • New RI must be equal or greater value
  • Region is fixed — cannot exchange to a different Region
  • May need to pay a "true-up" cost for higher value

💡 CRIs vs. Compute Savings Plans:
  • CRIs: Manual exchange required, capacity reservation available
  • Compute SP: Automatic application, no manual changes, higher discount
  • Key Insight: For most dynamic workloads, Compute Savings Plans are generally more suitable
🔄 Convertible RI 💰 up to 66% ✅ flexible
⏰

7.2.3 Scheduled RIs

📖 Complete Theory: The Recurring Capacity Model

Scheduled Reserved Instances are a specialized pricing option that allows you to reserve EC2 capacity for predictable, recurring time windows rather than for continuous use.

Core Use Case: Workloads that don't run 24/7 but do run on a regular, predictable schedule.

  • Applications that run during business hours (8am-5pm, weekdays)
  • Batch processing jobs at the end of each week
  • Monthly reporting or analytics workloads
  • Development environments used only during working hours

⚠️ Limitations:
  • Instance Types: C3, C4, C5, M4, R3 (very limited)
  • Regions: US-East, US-West, Europe (Ireland) only
  • Discount: Only ~5-10% below On-Demand
  • Minimum Utilization: 1,200 hours per year
  • Cannot: Cancel, modify, or resell after purchase

💡 How It Works: AWS terminates instances 3 minutes before the end of the scheduled time period to ensure capacity is available for other Scheduled Instances.
⏰ Scheduled RI 📋 recurring ⚡ 5-10% discount

Key Takeaways

  • Reserved Instances offer significant discounts (up to 75%) in exchange for a 1- or 3-year commitment to a specific instance configuration.

  • Standard RIs provide the highest discount but are rigid — fixed instance family, size, Region, OS, and tenancy.

  • Convertible RIs offer flexibility (can exchange instance family, OS, tenancy) at a lower discount (up to 66%).

  • Scheduled RIs are for predictable, recurring time windows (5-10% discount) — very limited use cases.

  • Regional RIs offer size flexibility but no capacity guarantee; Zonal RIs offer capacity reservation but no size flexibility.

  • Payment Options: All Upfront (highest discount), Partial Upfront (moderate), No Upfront (lowest) — pay more upfront, save more overall.

  • RIs do not auto-renew — after expiration, instances revert to On-Demand rates.

  • RIs cannot be cancelled — but can be modified, exchanged (Convertible), or sold (Standard) on the Marketplace.

Review Questions

Q1: What is the difference between a Standard RI and a Convertible RI?
Answer: A Standard RI offers the highest discount (up to 75%) but has fixed attributes and cannot be exchanged for a different instance family. A Convertible RI offers a lower discount (up to 66%) but allows you to exchange it for a different instance family, operating system, or tenancy during the term.
Q2: What are the three payment options for Reserved Instances?
Answer: The three payment options are: All Upfront (full payment at the start, highest discount), Partial Upfront (portion paid upfront, remaining at discounted hourly rate), and No Upfront (no upfront payment, discounted hourly rate billed monthly). The larger the upfront payment, the greater the discount.
Q3: What is the difference between a Regional RI and a Zonal RI?
Answer: A Regional RI applies the discount across all Availability Zones in the Region and offers instance size flexibility, but does not guarantee capacity. A Zonal RI is tied to a specific Availability Zone and provides capacity reservation, but does not offer instance size flexibility.
Q4: Can you cancel a Reserved Instance after purchasing it?
Answer: No, you cannot cancel a Reserved Instance purchase. However, if your needs change, you may be able to modify, exchange (Convertible RIs), or sell (Standard RIs on the RI Marketplace) your Reserved Instance.
Q5: What is the primary use case for Scheduled RIs?
Answer: Scheduled RIs are designed for workloads that do not run continuously but do run on a regular, predictable schedule. Examples include applications that run during business hours, batch processing jobs at the end of the week, or monthly reporting workloads. They offer a modest discount (~5-10%) and are limited to specific instance types and Regions.
Q6: What happens when a Reserved Instance expires?
Answer: Reserved Instances do not renew automatically. When they expire, you can continue using the EC2 instance without interruption, but you will be charged On-Demand rates until you terminate the instance or purchase a new Reserved Instance.
